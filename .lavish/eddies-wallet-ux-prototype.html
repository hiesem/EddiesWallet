02 · Annotated flow

The selected MVP story

A dashboard-first start now leads through an explicit path: setup, allowance, activity, and just-in-time education stay connected. The parent’s write event becomes Eddie’s read-only explanation.

Captain direction applied

Annotation intent: the parent owns the write; Eddie receives a reason. Borrowing is delayed until balance and saving have a shared vocabulary.

Why start with a boundary?

It prevents the product from being mistaken for a bank account or money-moving tool.

Why preview before confirm?

A parent can catch a wrong amount or direction before the virtual-credit snapshot changes.

Why delay borrowing?

“Owed” is easier to learn after Eddie has seen balance, activity, and saving work.

03 · Variation comparison

Same data, three different starts

A is the selected MVP spine. B and C remain intact and playable as information-architecture alternatives, not themes, so the next review can compare them without losing the original exploration.

A selected · B/C retained

A · Dashboard-first

Parent sees the state of the family space immediately, then records a moment.

  • →Best for a parent checking in quickly.
  • →Balance and sync lead the hierarchy.
  • →Risk: Eddie’s learning story is one tap away.

B · Journey-first

Both roles see a small “where we are” path and one suggested next step.

  • →Best for guided onboarding and teaching.
  • →Sequence makes education timing explicit.
  • →Risk: repeat parent actions may feel slower.

C · Pockets + jars

The primary mental model is “where virtual credits live”: Spend, Save, and Owed.

  • →Best for concrete, child-friendly conversations.
  • →Moves and goals become spatially visible.
  • →Risk: events and sync may be less prominent.
